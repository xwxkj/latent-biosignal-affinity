# Statistical methods for the Communications Psychology revision

The revised analysis treats patients or participants—not pairwise comparisons—as the independent sampling units. For each sample, latent similarity to eligible same-affinity samples was averaged and contrasted with latent similarity to eligible different-affinity samples. Comparisons involving the same patient or participant were excluded in all datasets. In CASE, comparisons involving the same video stimulus were additionally excluded. Sample-level contrasts were averaged within each patient or participant to obtain one independent-unit contrast, Δ, per unit.

The reported point estimate is the mean of the independent-unit Δ values. Uncertainty was estimated with a multinomial cluster bootstrap. Independent patients or participants received bootstrap multiplicities, the full affinity statistic was recomputed using the resulting cluster weights while within-unit comparisons remained excluded, and percentile intervals were reported at the 95% level. Statistical significance was assessed by restricted permutation. PTB-XL diagnostic labels were permuted across patients while preserving class counts. WESAD state labels and CASE affective labels were permuted within participants, preserving the repeated-measures structure and each participant's marginal label distribution. Both two-sided and directional permutation P values are saved; the two-sided value is recommended as the primary manuscript result.

PTB-XL retained at most one ECG per patient and included an age- and sex-adjusted sensitivity analysis. WESAD excluded all within-participant pairs and used both chest and wrist physiology. CASE excluded both within-participant and within-video pairs, included video-centred and leave-one-video-out sensitivity analyses, and controlled the secondary valence and arousal tests using the Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate procedure. Robustness was also evaluated across PCA dimensions of 4, 8 and 16 and across cosine, correlation and negative squared-Euclidean similarity measures.
