# Proof-of-concept results for Latent Biosignal Affinity

## PTB-XL pathological-affinity analysis

In the PTB-XL proof-of-concept analysis, ECG-derived latent biosignal similarity was higher for pairs sharing the same diagnostic superclass than for pairs from different diagnostic groups. Across 7,688 ECG records and 400,000 sampled pairs, same-diagnostic pairs showed higher latent similarity than different-diagnostic pairs (mean similarity: 0.1046 versus −0.0117; Δ = 0.1163; Cohen’s d = 0.2891; permutation p = 2.0 × 10−4). These results support the pathological-affinity component of the proposed latent biosignal affinity framework.

## WESAD stress/affect-affinity analysis

In the WESAD proof-of-concept analysis, wearable-derived latent biosignal similarity was higher for pairs sharing the same affective state than for pairs from different affective states. Same-affinity pairs showed higher latent similarity than different-affinity pairs (Δ = 0.3303; permutation p = 2.0 × 10−4), indicating that stress- and affect-related physiological states form a statistically detectable structure in wearable biosignal space. These results support the stress/affect-affinity component of the proposed framework.

## Overall interpretation

Together, the PTB-XL and WESAD analyses provide two independent proof-of-concept demonstrations of latent biosignal affinity. The PTB-XL result supports pathological affinity in ECG-derived representations, whereas the WESAD result supports stress/affect affinity in wearable multimodal physiological representations. These findings do not imply deterministic inference of individual traits or relationships. Rather, they show that group-level affinity labels can be statistically associated with similarity in latent biosignal spaces.
