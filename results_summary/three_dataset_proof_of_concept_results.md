# Three-dataset proof-of-concept results for Latent Biosignal Affinity

## PTB-XL pathological-affinity analysis

In the PTB-XL proof-of-concept analysis, ECG-derived latent biosignal similarity was higher for pairs sharing the same diagnostic superclass than for pairs from different diagnostic groups. Across 7,688 ECG records and 400,000 sampled pairs, same-diagnostic pairs showed higher latent similarity than different-diagnostic pairs (mean similarity: 0.1046 versus −0.0117; Δ = 0.1163; Cohen’s d = 0.2891; permutation p = 2.0 × 10−4). These results support the pathological-affinity component of the proposed latent biosignal affinity framework.

## WESAD stress/affect-affinity analysis

In the WESAD proof-of-concept analysis, wearable-derived latent biosignal similarity was higher for pairs sharing the same affective state than for pairs from different affective states. Same-affinity pairs showed higher latent similarity than different-affinity pairs (Δ = 0.3303; permutation p = 2.0 × 10−4). These results support the stress/affect-affinity component of the proposed framework.

## CASE psychological/affective-affinity analysis

In the CASE proof-of-concept analysis, we tested whether cross-subject physiological segments sharing the same valence-arousal quadrant were closer in latent biosignal space than segments from different quadrants. Across 330 subject-video segments and 52,635 cross-subject sampled pairs, same-quadrant pairs showed higher latent similarity than different-quadrant pairs (mean similarity: 0.0197 versus −0.0028; Δ = 0.0225; Cohen’s d = 0.0594; permutation p = 2.0 × 10−4). Similar but weaker effects were also observed for arousal binning (Δ = 0.0183; permutation p = 0.0014) and valence binning (Δ = 0.0157; permutation p = 0.0108). These results support a statistically detectable, although modest, psychological/affective-affinity structure in physiological biosignal space.

## Overall interpretation

Together, PTB-XL, WESAD and CASE provide three independent proof-of-concept demonstrations of latent biosignal affinity. PTB-XL supports pathological affinity in ECG-derived representations, WESAD supports stress/affect affinity in wearable multimodal physiological representations, and CASE supports cross-subject psychological/affective affinity in valence-arousal physiological states. These results do not imply deterministic inference of individual personality, relationship or disease. Rather, they show that group-level affinity labels can be statistically associated with similarity in latent biosignal spaces.
